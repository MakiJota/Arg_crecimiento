## Implications

### Test 3 (relative cut per city)

- **The cut is very different from city to city.** At p25 in 1990 (census 1991) it goes from
  2,374 p/km² (Rawson - Trelew) to 6,648 (Gran Buenos Aires), median 4,193. A single absolute
  cut such as 500 or 3,000 is strict in some cities and loose in others (`reglas/resumen.csv`,
  `reglas/REPORTE.md`).
- **Tract densities fall over time.** p25 is lower in 2020 (census 2022) than in 1990 in 24 of
  the 31 agglomerations: median 4,193 p/km² in 1990, 3,630 in 2020. So the same absolute cut drops
  more land in 2020 than in 1990. Part of the slower growth that tests 1 and 2 show at high
  absolute cuts is this general loss of density, not just sprawl into empty land.
- **Test 3 hardly changes growth.** At p25 it keeps 58-78 % of the Angel extent in 2020 (median
  71 %; 5,032 of 6,997 km² across the 31). Its 1990-2020 growth rate is within 0.65 points a
  year of Angel's in every city (from -0.62 to +0.55; median -0.06). A relative cut rescales every year
  by about the same proportion, so it changes the **size** of the city, not its **growth**.
  - If the question is "how much of the growth is sparse land", use the absolute cuts (tests 1
    and 2).
  - If the question is "what is the lived-in city in each year, comparably across cities", test 3
    fits better.

### The fill rule (open land inside the centre)

- **The first idea (80 % built around) does almost nothing.** GISD30 sees only about half the
  pixels in old centres. In Salta 1990 the interior open land mostly has 30-60 % built within
  150 m, so 80 % fills 1.1 of the 12.5 km² of interior open land. The 60 m gap rule fills 9.2 km²
  and leaves parks and large open blocks alone (`reglas/salta/REPORTE.md`).
- **Size of the fill.** The central rule (gaps up to 60 m, more than 200 m from the edge, never on
  water) turns 18-42 % of each city's open land into built-up in 1990 (386 of 1,191 km² across
  the 31) and 18-39 % in 2020 (705 of 2,115 km²) (`reglas/resumen.csv`).
- **It is not specific to 1990.** The share filled is about the same in 1990 and 2020 (median
  29 % and 30 %). The rule mostly picks up the 30 m texture of GISD30 in any year: streets, tree
  cover, courtyards. Under-detection in the older imagery is only part of it.
- **The premise check of test 2 barely moves.** Persons per km² of built-up land are higher in
  1990 than in 2020 (median ratio 1.47). With the fill in dense tracts the ratio is 1.39. Filling
  gaps explains only a small part of the high early densities; most of it is real densification
  or loss of density, or under-detection the gap rule cannot see (whole blocks missed).
- **Commercial cores.** Centres with few residents (banks, shops, offices) can fall below the
  density cut, so tests 1-3 drop their fringe (red patches in the middle of Salta at p25). The
  fill **without** the dense-tract condition keeps those gaps. In practice the cores are almost
  all built-up, so little is at stake there (see the visual check).
- **What it changes in the measures.**
  - The Angel extent: nothing. The fill moves land from open to built inside it.
  - Built-up km²: +2 to +33 % in 1990 depending on the city (median +12 %).
  - Persons per km² built: they drop accordingly.
  - Tests 1-3: they change only when the fill applies in sparse tracts. Salta at p25 with that
    fill: 184.3 km² in 2020 instead of 150.7 (`reglas/salta/REPORTE.md`).

## Visual check

A separate agent checked the maps against the imagery, without editing anything. Full review: [reglas/REVISION_VISUAL.md](reglas/REVISION_VISUAL.md).

- **What it covered.** Nine agglomerations plus two spots in Gran Buenos Aires: Salta, Tucumán,
  Catamarca, Neuquén, Santa Fe, Corrientes, Rawson - Trelew, Mendoza and San Juan.
- **How.** About 55 screenshots of the viewer over the Landsat of each year, and over present-day
  Esri imagery where there is no Landsat, at 1990 and 2020, centre and periphery, with the fill
  rule in dense tracts only and in all tracts.

Findings:

- **Compact cities are mapped well.** In Corrientes (the best case), Tucumán, Santa Fe, the
  centre of Salta, Trelew proper and the cores of Mendoza and San Juan:
  - built-up follows the urban fabric;
  - the density cut drops parks, wetlands and large fields;
  - the 200 m edge ring follows the real edge;
  - about 70-90 % of the fill falls on built fabric: tree-covered streets, courtyards, canopy over
    blocks.
- **The 60 m gap is right.** 90 m is clearly too aggressive: in Salta 1990 almost everything
  fills, the river included, and every channel in Nordelta fills. 30 m is too timid: tree-lined
  streets in dense 1990 cores stay as holes.
- **Error 1: rivers and canals were filled as ribbons.** Seen on the Río Salí, the Arenales and
  the Tavella canal, the banks of the Limay, the Chubut, and Nordelta and Puerto Madero.
  **Fixed** with the water mask (JRC Global Surface Water + OpenStreetMap rivers and canals).
  The mask covers the Salí and the urban canals of Tucumán, which JRC alone missed.
- **Error 2: irrigated farmland.** GISD30 marks irrigated fields as built, and the fill joins
  them. **Rawson - Trelew is not valid**: its "city" is about 30 km of the lower Chubut valley,
  which also explains its very low persons per km² built. The same, smaller, in:
  - the Maipú and Guaymallén vineyards (Mendoza; 40-50 % of the fill there is on farmland);
  - Santa Lucía (San Juan);
  - the chacras of Neuquén;
  - the horticultural plots south of Salta.

  **Not fixed**: it needs a cropland mask. The viewer shows a warning on these five
  agglomerations.
- **Fill only in dense tracts, or in all.**
  - *Dense tracts only* turns the fill on farms and rivers red, which is right. But in 1990 it
    also drops real low-density built land: Yerba Buena, Valle Viejo, the outer belts of
    Corrientes and San Juan, southwest Santa Fe.
  - *All tracts* keeps that land but also keeps the errors.
  - The reviewer suggests a hybrid (no condition above the median density, condition below it).
    Not implemented.
- **Commercial cores.** The reviewer found none dropped by the density rule in the cities
  checked: they are almost all built-up, so the rule barely acts there. The red patches in
  Salta's centre at p25 are in tracts below the cut; most of their land is built-up, so little
  is lost.
- **Built outside the city (yellow).** Mostly real: separate towns, houses along roads, airport
  runways, levees. Little noise.

## MapBiomas 2020 (added 2026-11-17)

MapBiomas Argentina collection 2, 2020, against the GISD30 city of 2020, pixel by pixel, in the
31 agglomerations (`mapbiomas/REPORTE.md`, `mapbiomas/resumen.csv`).

- **What GISD30 calls built-up, MapBiomas mostly calls urban.** The median is 95.5 % of the
  built-up of the city. The exceptions are the ones the visual check found:
  - Rawson - Trelew: 29.4 % urban, 48.8 % crops or pasture;
  - Salta: 74.4 % urban, 19.5 % crops;
  - Neuquén - Plottier: 84.3 % urban, 12.6 % crops.

  It is an independent confirmation that the problem there is irrigated farmland in GISD30, not
  the tests.
- **The fill is accurate in dense tracts and not elsewhere.**
  - *Dense tracts only* (above the test 3 cut): MapBiomas calls urban 93 % of the filled land
    (median), and at least 85 % in 26 of the 31. The lowest are La Rioja (65 %), Mar del Plata
    (71 %) and San Nicolás (77 %).
  - *All tracts*: the median falls to 81 %, and to 43 % in Salta and 9 % in Rawson - Trelew,
    where it falls on crops.

  So keep "only in dense tracts" on, except where the 1990 suburbs matter (see the visual check).
- **The fill brings GISD30 closer to MapBiomas.** The overlap (Jaccard) between built-up and
  MapBiomas urban rises with the fill in 29 of the 31 agglomerations.
- **Fringe and captured land are mostly not urban for MapBiomas.** The median is 37 %. That is
  consistent with Angel: they are open land, though part of it is built and unseen.
- **MapBiomas urban outside the Angel city.** About 1,167 of its 6,736 km² across the 31 is
  outside the Angel city: separate towns and dispersed development, the yellow of the maps.
- **Caveat.** MapBiomas is another classification, not ground truth. It can call a tree-covered
  neighbourhood pasture.

  and Salta.
